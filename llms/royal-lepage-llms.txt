# Royal LePage

> Canada's largest residential real estate brokerage brand, founded in Toronto in 1913 as A.E. LePage and operated today under franchise and corporately-owned brokerages by Bridgemarq Real Estate Services (TSX: BRE), an affiliate of Brookfield. Roughly 20,000 REALTORS across 600+ offices. Royal LePage publishes NO developer API. Its consumer search at royallepage.ca is a downstream consumer of Canadian listing data licensed from the Canadian Real Estate Association's Data Distribution Facility (DDF), and its legal notice forbids automated access.

## API posture

- No developer portal, no documented API, no OpenAPI/Swagger, no OData `$metadata`, no GraphQL, no AsyncAPI, no webhooks, no MCP server, no SDKs, no Postman collection.
- No RESO Web API or RESO Data Dictionary certification. RESO is a US NAR-adjacent regime; Canadian syndication runs through CREA's single national DDF instead.
- `developer.royallepage.ca`, `developers.royallepage.ca`, `api.royallepage.ca` and `status.royallepage.ca` are NOT developer properties — they are wildcard DNS records pointing at the kvCORE agent-website platform behind a Cloudflare challenge.
- `rlpnetwork.com` is a members-only agent/franchisee portal that 301s every unknown path to `/login`; the 200s it returns for `/.well-known/*`, `/api-docs`, and `/graphql` are login pages, not discovery documents.

## For an agent or crawler

- Automated access is contractually prohibited: the legal notice bans "spiders, robots, crawlers, data mining tools, or the like" and forbids scraping, storing, or redistributing site data. There is no API carve-out.
- Registering an account creates a Virtual Office Website (VOW) relationship restricting data to personal, non-commercial use.
- Programmatic access to the underlying Canadian listing record is obtained from CREA under a DDF licence (REALTOR/CREA membership or an authorised technology-provider arrangement) — not from Royal LePage.
